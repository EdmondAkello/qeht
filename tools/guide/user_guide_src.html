0What QEHT does

QEHT turns a digital elevation model (DEM) into the drainage quantities a road or catchment study needs: conditioned terrain, flow direction and accumulation, streams, catchments, longest flow paths and their slopes, crossings along a road, and the soils, runoff, rainfall, erosion and time-of-concentration inputs of a design-flood calculation. Everything runs inside QGIS, using only NumPy and the GDAL bindings that QGIS already ships. It starts no external program and needs no extra Python package, so it works on managed workstations where tools that start external programs are blocked.

Terrain and drainage

DEM preparation and checks, priority-flood fill, D8 with Barnes (2014) flat resolution, accumulation, Strahler streams, catchments, longest flow paths and four named slopes.

Road drainage

Crossing candidates with chainage, embankment burning, the ground profile, a coverage check that proposes missing crossings, channel sections, floodplain width, mapped-river check.

Design inputs

Soil texture and USLE K from any soil source, curve numbers and Rational C, rainfall zones, five time-of-concentration methods side by side, land-cover scenarios.

Erosion

SPI, TWI, LS and RUSLE with source-tagged factors, severity classes, erosion along the road and a side-drain siltation indicator.

Uncertainty and QA

Flat-method sensitivity per crossing, DEM-error Monte Carlo ranges of area and Tc, undeclared-NoData and resampling audits, provenance in every output.

Delivery

One-click pipeline into one folder: layers, tables, an HTML run report, KMZ for Google Earth, an XLSX workbook and a self-describing GeoPackage.

Rules the plugin keeps. No engineering value ships as a default when it is not known: a land-cover proxy is labelled PROXY, a relation used outside its origin is labelled ESTIMATE, and both labels travel into every table, report and package. Where a design method needs a choice (which slope, which Tc method, which flat method), QEHT reports the alternatives and records which was used; it does not choose for you.

1Overview

QEHT is a QGIS Processing provider. Its tools appear in the Processing Toolbox under Engineering Hydrology and behave like any other Processing algorithm: they validate their inputs, run in batch mode, chain in the Graphical Modeler, log to the history and can be called from Python with processing.run().

A typical workflow

  1. Prepare the DEM: one product, projected in metres, resampled bilinearly, NoData declared (section 3 and 10).
  2. Run the pipeline with the DEM and the road centreline, and stop after the crossing candidates (section 5).
  3. Review the candidates: accept, reject, move or add crossings; then run again with the reviewed layer (section 6).
  4. Read the run report: DEM checks, flat-sensitive crossings, coverage findings, uncertainty ranges and warnings (section 8).
  5. Adopt or delete proposed crossings and relink the package if you edited it (section 29).
  6. Take the numbers into design: the characteristics table, the Tc table and the XLSX workbook, with the slope and Tc method you use stated in the design report.

2Installing and updating

From the QGIS plugin repository

  1. Open Plugins → Manage and Install Plugins…
  2. Search for QEHT and press Install (or Upgrade).
  3. Open Processing → Toolbox and expand Engineering Hydrology.

From a ZIP file

Use Plugins → Manage and Install Plugins → Install from ZIP with qeht-{{VERSION}}.zip. The zip holds one folder, qeht; keep that name, because the tests and the plugin import qeht.core.

RequirementsQGIS 3.34 or later, including QGIS 4 (Qt6), on Windows, macOS or Linux. Nothing else: no SAGA, GRASS, WhiteboxTools, TauDEM or pip packages. Matplotlib, which QGIS ships on most installs, is used only for the KMZ legend and the optional profile charts.

Memory

QEHT holds the whole grid in memory. Every tool estimates its peak before it runs and warns when the estimate approaches the free RAM. As a guide, the routing steps need about 50–170 bytes per cell: a 4,000 × 4,000 DEM (16 million cells) needs 1–3 GB. For a national mosaic, clip to the road's contributing area first (section 20).

3Preparing the inputs

The DEM

Every result depends on the DEM, so most of the checks in QEHT are about it. A DEM QEHT can trust is:

  • Projected, in metres. A geographic DEM (degrees) is refused before any computation. Prepare DEM for hydrology reprojects to the local UTM zone (section 10).
  • One product at its native resolution. FABDEM, Copernicus GLO-30 and ALOS AW3D30 are 30 m. Mixing products or exporting finer than native adds no information.
  • Resampled bilinearly, never nearest neighbour. Nearest neighbour repeats whole rows and columns wherever the output grid is finer than the source. The repeats create exact ties and artificial flats, and routing across them is decided by the flat method rather than the terrain.
  • With its NoData declared. A block of zeros with no NoData in the header is read as a sea at 0 m that the whole catchment drains into.

When the audit reports undeclared NoData, give the value in Treat this DEM value as NoData (pipeline) or Treat this value as NoData (Fill depressions). When it reports repeated rows or columns in the input, the damage happened before QEHT: get the native product and prepare it again. A FABDEM export from Earth Engine at a scale slightly finer than its native 1″ grid (for example 0.000269°) repeats about one row and column in 33; export in the native projection and scale.

The road centreline

A line layer, in any CRS (it is transformed to the DEM's). Chainage runs along the parts in layer order and the digitising direction from the Start chainage; tick Reverse chainage direction to run it the other way. A rough digitised centreline is enough before the geometric design exists: crossings, chainages and the ground profile are then provisional and are re-run on the design alignment later.

The log lists every step with its warnings. Read it once: warnings about the DEM, flat-sensitive crossings and inputs outside a method's range are repeated in the run report, but the log also shows the memory estimate and the time per step.

6Reviewing crossings and running again

QEHT finds where streams cross the road. It does not know where the culverts are, which streams the road actually blocks, or which crossings a designer will combine. Review the candidates before the full run:

  1. Run with Run = Stop after crossing candidates. The folder holds layers/crossing_candidates.gpkg and settings.json.
  2. Open the candidates over imagery or survey. Set status to accepted or rejected; move a point onto the culvert; add points where there is a structure QEHT missed. Candidates you leave as candidate are ignored when any is accepted; when none is accepted, the recommended ones (recommended = 1) are used.
  3. Run again with Re-run from settings = the first run's settings.json, Crossings = the edited layer and Run = Full run. Every other parameter comes from the settings file; a new output folder overrides the old one.

A candidate you move by more than two cells is snapped from its new position, and its chainage is taken from the road, exactly like a point you add. A candidate you leave in place keeps the outlet cell QEHT found for it.

Your own pour pointsAny point layer works as Crossings: surveyed culvert positions, a structure inventory, points digitised from imagery. The pipeline numbers them X001, X002 … along the chainage (downstream first without a road). To keep your own numbering as outlet_uid, run Build design hydrology package with ID attribute set (Part F). Points are snapped to the nearest stream cell within 5 cells; check snap_dist_m in the crossings layer for points that moved far.

8The run report

report/run_report.html opens in any browser and prints to PDF for an appendix. It reads the package only; nothing is recalculated, so the report always matches the data.

The sections that follow are, in order:

  • Crossing schedule: one row per crossing with chainage, area, flow-path length, 10–85 slope, catchment slope, curve number, rain zone, rainfall and the flat check.
  • DEM checks: the NoData and resampling audits, the flat-method check (section 11) and channel sections of low quality.
  • DEM uncertainty, Land-cover scenario, Check against mapped drainage, Time of concentration and Drainage coverage along the road, when those blocks ran.
  • Inputs and provenance: the DEM path and SHA-256, the QEHT version, every dataset and lookup, and the labels of proxies and estimates.
  • Warnings, the curve number lookup used and the list of output files.

9KMZ and XLSX exports

Export package to KMZ / XLSX (Workflow), and the pipeline by default, write two files for people without a GIS. Both writers are pure Python.

KMZ for Google Earth. Coordinates are transformed to WGS 84. Existing crossings are blue and proposed ones orange; each has a card with its location and chainage, catchment area, slopes, elevations, soil, curve number, rain zone, Tc by every method with its validity, the channel section and the erosion values. Catchments are translucent polygons, the road carries kilometre posts, and coverage findings, channel sections, side-drain siltation reaches and the relief quicklook are separate folders that can be switched on.

XLSX workbook. Five sheets: Catchment characteristics, Time of concentration, Coverage findings, Run metadata and Field dictionary. The characteristics sheet runs in design order (identification, area, flow path, elevations, the four slopes, segments, channel slopes, Tc, CN and C, rainfall, soil, widths, flags), with units in row 2 and panes frozen at B3. Every column is always written, so the layout is fixed; the column order is one list in core/report/xlsx_layout.py and can be matched to a house workbook.

10Prepare DEM for hydrology

Most bad runs start before QEHT: a DEM reprojected with nearest neighbour, an export finer than the native grid, or NoData zeros the file does not declare. Prepare DEM for hydrology does the preparation in-process.

  1. Mosaic. The tiles in DEM raster(s) become one virtual mosaic. They must be one product in one CRS.
  2. Clip. To Clip to polygons or Clip to extent, widened by the Clip buffer (2,000 m), so catchments that start outside the area of interest are not cut.
  3. Reproject. Target CRS = Auto picks the WGS 84 / UTM zone of the extent centre anywhere on Earth (UPS beyond 84° N and 80° S; the Norway and Svalbard zone exceptions apply). Or choose any projected CRS, such as a national grid. Resampling is bilinear (recommended) or cubic; nearest neighbour is not offered. The Cell size defaults to the native cell converted to metres.
  4. Audit. The input and the output are checked for undeclared NoData and repeated rows and columns. Repeats in the input mean the damage is upstream.
  5. Record. The output is float32 with NoData −9999. The DEM source and the preparation record are written into the GeoTIFF tags, and the package reads them into its metadata. The optional JSON log records the inputs, CRS, cell size, resampling, both audits and an estimate of the native grid.

11Fill, flow direction and flats

Fill depressions

Closed depressions are filled by priority flood (Barnes, Lehman and Mulla 2014). Most are DEM noise; some are real (a crater, a quarry, a pond behind an embankment).

  • Minimum slope across filled surfaces (0): leave at 0 for a classic fill. 0.0001 adds a small gradient across filled flats so that routing has a direction, which largely removes the flat-method question.
  • Fill depth (QA): an optional raster of how much each cell was raised. A long, deep fill along a road is an embankment the DEM treats as a dam: burn the crossings (section 15) rather than accept the pond.

D8 flow direction

Each cell drains to the neighbour of steepest descent, drop divided by distance (the diagonal distance for diagonal neighbours). Ties are broken by a fixed order (S, W, N, E, then the diagonals), so the result is deterministic. Directions are written in the standard D8 codes (E = 1, SE = 2, S = 4, SW = 8, W = 16, NW = 32, N = 64, NE = 128; 0 = sink or edge outlet), so a grid from another GIS can be used in QEHT's accumulation and catchment tools, and the reverse.

Flats: the one choice that changes catchments

After filling, flat areas have no downhill neighbour, and the flat method decides where they drain:

  • Barnes 2014 convergent (default since 0.13): flow drains away from higher ground and towards the flat's outlet, so it converges as on real terrain. QEHT reproduces Barnes' own RichDEM implementation cell for cell.
  • Toward lower terrain: each flat cell takes the shortest path to the outlet. Simpler, and it produces parallel flow lines across large flats.

On 80 random 15 km areas across Kenya, each on ALOS AW3D30 and FABDEM, Barnes agreed best with the independent TauDEM (stream F1 0.969 against 0.948 for toward-lower; 0.870 against 0.733 on whole-metre flats), which is why it is the default. BENCHMARK.md in the repository has the design and the tables.

Check flat-sensitive crossingsCatchment and flow path characteristics, the package and the pipeline route every crossing under both methods and flag flat_sensitive = 1 where the area differs by more than 10 %. A flagged catchment is decided by flats or ties, not terrain: check it against a map, imagery or the site before using it.

12Accumulation and streams

Flow accumulation counts the cells upstream of each cell (a ridge cell reads 0, the standard D8 convention), or their area, or a weight raster. It is a single topological pass with no recursion, so it runs on any grid that fits in memory. Cells in a cycle, which a valid drainage tree never has, are reported rather than dropped.

Stream network and Strahler order thresholds accumulation by cell count or contributing area and orders the network by Strahler's rules on the stream cells only. Stream network to polylines traces the raster network into reaches between sources, junctions and outlets, each with order, length, drop, slope and head_type.

ThresholdGive the threshold as an area in engineering work: it means the same thing on a 30 m and a 12.5 m DEM. 0.5–1 km² suits road drainage at 30 m; much smaller areas are below what a 30 m DEM resolves.

13Catchments and flow paths

Delineate catchments from pour points, Longest flow path and Catchment and flow path characteristics take a pour-point layer. Each pour point is first snapped to the nearest stream cell within the Snap radius (5 cells). The stream for snapping is the Snap-to-stream threshold (200 cells by default): set it low enough to include the tributary a crossing sits on. Snapping to the cell of largest accumulation instead pulls road crossings onto the trunk stream next to them: on the validation site that inflated one catchment forty times.

outlet_uid

Every catchment and flow path carries outlet_uid, assigned once per run: from a pour-point attribute you choose (existing culvert numbers, for example), or sequential with your prefix (X001, X002 …) along the chainage or downstream first. The three layers of a run share it, so they join on it. outlet_id, the feature id of the pour point, changes when a layer is edited or saved again: never join on it.

Flow-path segments and shape

The longest flow path is split at the channel head, the first cell that reaches the stream threshold: lfp_overland_m and lfp_channel_m with their slopes (they add up to lfp_length_m), and the overland part as sheet flow up to a cap (100 m) plus shallow concentrated flow. These are the inputs of the Kerby and TR-55 segment methods. Shape and network indices (perimeter, form factor, elongation and circularity ratios, drainage density, stream frequency, highest Strahler order) are given for information.

Raw DEM for elevationsRouting uses the conditioned DEM; reported elevations and slopes come from the raw one, or heights inside filled depressions read as the fill surface. Give the Raw DEM to Catchment characteristics; the package and the pipeline always use it.

Part DRoad drainage

From a road centreline to a reviewed set of crossings: where streams cross the road, where water ponds against it, what the channel looks like downstream, whether the DEM agrees with the map, and how far DEM error can move each catchment.

14Road crossing candidates

Road crossing candidates intersects every D8 flow link of the stream network near the road with the centreline. These are the same segments QEHT's stream polylines are made of, so no crossing is lost to a digitising gap between a stream line and the road.

Each candidate carries its chainage, the crossing angle (90 = square), the contributing area, the Strahler order, the reach id, the side the flow comes from (L or R of the chainage direction) and status = candidate. Nothing is deleted.

Parallel flow and clusters

A stream that runs beside the road inside the Corridor half-width (30 m) for at least the Minimum parallel length (100 m) crosses the centreline many times on a DEM. Those candidates share a cluster, the stream is written to the parallel reaches layer (where a side drain has to carry the water to the crossing), and the most downstream candidate of the cluster, the one with the largest area, gets recommended = 1. Candidates closer than the Cluster merge distance also share a cluster (0 = off).

A crossing used for a cluster covers the cluster's other intersections: the coverage check (section 17) does not propose crossings on them.

15Burning crossings through embankments

A DSM, a LiDAR or survey DEM, or a fine DEM of a built road shows the embankment as a dam, and filling turns the valley behind it into a lake. Burn crossings through embankments cuts a straight breach across the road at each crossing:

  • The breach runs perpendicular to the road between two points a half-length (30 m) either side of the centreline. Each end moves to the lowest cell within the search radius (2 cells) on its own side.
  • Cells on the line are lowered to a straight grade from the upstream to the downstream end (at least 0.0001 so it drains). Nothing is raised and nothing outside the breach changes.
  • The breach log lists every crossing with cells cut, maximum cut and volume; a crossing with no ground above the grade (no embankment in the DEM) is logged with 0 cells. Give the log to Build design hydrology package and it is stored in the package.

Then fill, route and accumulate the burned DEM. The pipeline does this when Burn crossings through embankments is ticked. On a 30 m global DEM the road is usually not in the terrain, and burning changes nothing; check the fill depth along the road first.

16Alignment ground profile

Alignment ground profile stations the centreline every 10 m: the DEM ground (bilinear), the filled-DEM level and the ponding depth the fill removed, the contributing area (the largest within one cell), the stream crossings with their Strahler order and the longitudinal ground slope. The levels are provisional before the geometric design exists, and a check on DEM height bias afterwards. The package writes the same profile as alignment_profile.

With a road, Build design hydrology package and the pipeline check the road for drainage with no crossing, from the DEM alone; Drainage coverage check along a road gives the same findings without delineating anything.

18Channel sections and floodplain width

Channel section downstream of each crossing

The package and the pipeline cut a cross-section of the natural channel 30 m downstream of every crossing, for the tailwater rating, the waterway and the sediment-continuity check. It lies on the D8 path, perpendicular to the flow (taken over two cells up and down, which smooths D8's 45° steps), sampled bilinearly every half cell over ±150 m, positive on the right looking downstream.

  • xs_bed_m: the lowest ground within one cell of the centre.
  • xs_bankfull_w_m, xs_bankfull_d_m: bank-full width and depth by break of slope. A bank top is where the side slope falls below 1:20, or to less than half the slope behind; the lower bank governs.
  • xs_w_0p5_m, xs_w_1p0_m, xs_w_2p0_m: widths below bed + 0.5, 1 and 2 m, which hold when there is no clear bank; side slopes as H:V; the transect itself as xs_station_elev_json and the layer xs_transects.
  • xs_quality: low for a bank-full width under 3 cells or no bank on either side, medium for one bank or a transect cut by NoData, high otherwise; xs_note says why.

Floodplain width at large crossings

At every crossing of at least 10 km², QEHT measures how wide the valley floor is along the road: the run of the ground profile around the lowest ground within 50 m of the crossing where the ground is below bed + 0.5, 1 and 2 m (fp_w_0p5_m …), and the same by height above nearest drainage (fp_hand_w_*). A width that runs off the end of the profile is a lower bound and fp_note says so. The indicator informs the split of the check flood between the main structure and relief culverts; it is not a flood level.

19Check against mapped drainage

Did the DEM put the river where the map does? Give Mapped waterways (OpenStreetMap waterways, HydroRIVERS or a national layer, with an optional name field). QEHT compares them with the DEM streams at a comparison threshold matched to the map's scale (1 km² by default; about 10 km² for HydroRIVERS) within a tolerance of 60 m.

  • Network agreement: precision (share of DEM stream length on the map), recall (share of mapped length found by the DEM) and F1, overall and per catchment.
  • Per crossing: distance to the nearest mapped waterway, its name and map_agrees (1 within the tolerance, 0 up to 1 km, empty beyond or for crossings below the comparison threshold).
  • Divergence reaches: DEM stream runs of at least 10 cells that leave the mapped course, with their length and the area flowing down them. A large area here is how a catchment that switches between crossings shows up.

20Large DEMs: clip to the contributing area

A national mosaic will not fit in a laptop's memory, and clipping by hand can cut catchments. Clip DEM to the road's contributing area (or the pipeline option) finds the area that drains to the road on a coarse copy and reads only that window at full resolution:

  1. The coarse copy takes the minimum of each block, so valleys are kept; the block size keeps the copy under 4 million cells.
  2. The copy is filled and routed; every coarse cell that drains to the road buffer (200 m) is kept.
  3. That area is widened by the margin (1,000 m plus two coarse cells), and its bounding box (or the mask) is read from the full DEM.

The tool reports the memory estimate before and after. The clipped DEM is tagged, and the package then gives every catchment clip_edge: 1 when it touches the edge of the clip, with a warning to enlarge the margin.

21DEM uncertainty at crossings

One catchment area from one DEM hides how far the DEM's error can move it. DEM uncertainty at crossings (or the package and pipeline option) runs a Monte Carlo:

  1. A spatially correlated Gaussian error is added to the raw DEM: standard deviation σ, correlation exp(−r²/L²) with L = 90 m by default.
  2. The DEM is filled, routed and accumulated again, and each crossing's pour point is snapped to the stream.
  3. Area, longest flow path, 10–85 slope and Kirpich Tc are measured. This repeats N = 50 times (seed 2026, recorded, so the run repeats exactly).

Vertical error, with no default. AW3D30: 4.4 m, the RMSE of the JAXA validation (Tadono et al. 2016). FABDEM: 2.5 m, an ESTIMATE from the mean absolute errors of Hawker et al. (2022), 1.1 m built-up to 2.9 m forest. Or your own value.

Per crossing the run gives P10, P50, P90 and the coefficient of variation of each value (unc_*), the share of runs where the outlet lost its stream (unc_lost_pct) and the share where the area moved by more than 25 % (unc_switch_pct), the sign of a catchment that switches between crossings. For the three largest crossings the run is repeated at 0.5 × and 2 × the correlation length. A crossing is not assessed when the unperturbed DEM has no stream within the snap radius, or when the snap reaches another channel (the snapped area differs by more than 25 % from the crossing's own): proposed sag crossings below the stream threshold are the usual case.

The time per realisation is reported before the loop (under 1 s for the 0.55-million-cell demonstration DEM). Clip a large DEM first.

22Soils and USLE K

Soil, rainfall and runoff parameters for catchments (and the soil inputs of the package and the pipeline) adds a soil block to every catchment: area-weighted topsoil sand, silt, clay, organic carbon, coarse fragments and bulk density; USDA texture; dominant FAO drainage class; USLE K; a hydrologic soil group; and the share of the catchment covered by soil data.

K and the hydrologic soil group

K is the Williams (EPIC) relation in SI units (t·ha·h/(ha·MJ·mm)), with the Renard Dg-based value as an alternative and the coarse-fragment factor reported separately. Because the relation is non-linear, K is computed per component or per cell and then averaged, never from averaged texture. The hydrologic soil group, when no HSG data is given, is a PROXY from texture and drainage (A: sand, loamy sand; B: sandy loam, loam, silt loam; C: sandy clay loam; D: clay loams and clays; poorly drained soils move to D). soil_hsg_source and usle_k_source say which was used.

Treat K as an estimateAcross Kenya, SOTWIS-derived K has a median of 0.029 against 0.022–0.024 for the ESDAC global K rasters over the same cells, with a correlation of only 0.24–0.40. The two are independent estimates at very different resolutions. Report which one you used.

23Curve number, Rational C and scenarios

With a land-cover raster in ESA WorldCover classes, each cell's land cover and hydrologic soil group give a curve number from USDA TR-55 Table 2-2, for the chosen condition (fair, good or poor). The catchment value cn_ii is the cell mean, exported at the antecedent moisture condition you choose (cn_export, AMC I, II or III).

  • The match from WorldCover classes to TR-55 covers (tree → woods, shrub → brush, grass → pasture, crop → row crops, built-up → commercial 85 % impervious, bare → fallow) is a PROXY, flagged in runoff_json. Replace it with your own Curve number lookup CSV (class, A, B, C, D).
  • Rational C comes only from a lookup you supply (Rational C lookup CSV); none ships.
  • Land-cover shares lc_pct_* are always given with a land-cover raster.

Land-cover scenarios

Give a second raster with the same classes (a planned development, a future land-use map) as Land-cover scenario, with a name. It runs through the same lookups and the same soil groups as the baseline, so the change comes from land cover only: cn_ii_scn, d_cn, rational_c_scn, d_rational_c, scenario land-cover shares, and with an erosion folder the scenario C factor and soil loss (A scaled per cell by Cscn/C, which holds because A = R K LS C P).

24Rainfall

  • Rainfall zones: a polygon layer and its name field. Per catchment the dominant zone (rain_zone; ties go to the name that sorts first, so the result does not depend on layer order), its share and the share of every zone. A warning lists catchments whose dominant zone covers less than 80 %: pick their design zone by hand.
  • Mean annual rainfall: a raster in mm/yr (a CHIRPS climatology, for example), bilinear to the DEM grid: map_mm. Values that cannot be annual millimetres (mm/day, tenths of mm, negative) are refused.
  • R from rainfall (optional): RUSLE R per cell from the rainfall by Renard and Freimund (1994) or Lo et al. (1985), labelled an ESTIMATE. Neither relation comes from East Africa; an erosivity raster such as GloREDa is better.

26Erosion indices and RUSLE

Erosion indices and RUSLE soil loss writes one folder with the terrain indices, an optional soil-loss raster, severity classes (with colours, class names and a QGIS style) and erosion_run.json, which records every factor's source and every setting.

  • Indices on the raw DEM: Horn slope; specific catchment area As = (upslope cells + 1) × cell area / cell size; SPI = As tan β; TWI = ln(As / tan β); LS by Moore and Burch (m = 0.4, or m by slope class) or Desmet and Govers.
  • RUSLE A = R K LS C P (t/ha/yr). Each factor is a raster, a single value or a dataset: R from a raster (GloREDa recommended) or the rainfall estimate; K from the soil inputs; C from WorldCover through an editable class lookup, flagged as a PROXY; P = 1 unless given. If R, K or C is missing, no soil loss is computed: QEHT never invents a factor and falls back to LS-only classes labelled "terrain potential".
  • Classes. SPI by percentiles of the extent or fixed ln(SPI) 0 / 5 / 10; RUSLE by 5 / 12 / 25 / 50 or 5 / 10 / 20 / 40 t/ha/yr, or your own breaks; the combined class is the higher of the two unless you give a 5 × 5 matrix. class_extents.csv gives the area per class, split into channel and hillslope cells.
  • Sediment transport index: sti_overland.tif, Moore and Wilson (1992) on overland cells (channels NoData), a relative indicator of where slopes deliver sediment. It is not used in soil loss, the classes or the composite.

With the erosion folder, the package adds an erosion block: per catchment the mean and P90 soil loss, LS, channel ln(SPI), area-weighted K, C and P, class shares, gross soil loss, the sediment delivery ratio (SDR = 0.565 A−0.125) and sediment volume; per crossing the ln(SPI) at and above the outlet, local slope, LS and TWI, a deposition indicator on the approach channel, and the hydrodynamic impact score 0.4·SPI + 0.3·RUSLE + 0.3·sediment (Low / Moderate / High / Severe) with an indicative mitigation, following the A14 corridor study (Akello and Omosa 2025).

27Erosion along the road and side drains

Sample erosion along alignment reads the erosion folder and a road centreline. Stations every 10 m carry the maximum and mean ln(SPI), LS, soil loss and TWI on each side within a 50 m buffer; consecutive stations with the same worst class form reaches whose lengths add up to the road length.

Side-drain siltation. Give the flow direction (and the raw DEM for the flag) and each station also measures the slope that feeds each side drain: the cells sampled 10–50 m out whose D8 path reaches the road strip without entering a channel. sti_p50_lhs, sti_p90_lhs and the same for the right side come from the overland STI; classes (low, moderate, high, very high) are relative to this corridor's own stations; siltation_lhs / _rhs = 1 where the class is high or very high and the longitudinal slope is under 1 %. The package writes the reaches as the layer corridor_sti.

Rules

  • The three main layers share outlet_uid, so any reader links them by key, never by row order or position.
  • A projected, metric CRS is required; a DEM in degrees is refused before any computation.
  • Empty or duplicate IDs, and two pour points snapping to the same cell, stop the run with a list.
  • Missing values are NULL, never 0. acc_at_outlet_km2, read from the accumulation raster, equals area_km2 for full catchments: a built-in check.
  • The schema only grows. Fields and layers are added under qeht-heas-1; renaming or removing one would be qeht-heas-2, and the validator refuses unknown major versions.
  • The file is written with the Python standard library, so it is byte-for-byte the same on every platform; the tests check it with GDAL's GeoPackage validator.

IDs. ID attribute keeps your own numbering (culvert numbers from an inventory, for example). Without it IDs are sequential with the ID prefix (X): along the chainage when the crossings have one, otherwise downstream first (the largest area is 001). Proposed crossings use the Proposed prefix (P).

HEAS, a separate hydrology–hydraulics design tool, imports the package directly. It is not required: the package opens in QGIS like any GeoPackage, and the pipeline also writes every layer as CSV.

29Editing crossings: renumber and relink

After you delete, move or add crossings in a package, or adopt proposed ones, Renumber and relink exchange package writes a new package:

  1. Unmoved crossings keep their outlet cell; moved and added ones are snapped to the stream.
  2. Crossings are ordered by chainage (recomputed from the road for moved and added points) or downstream first.
  3. IDs are re-issued gaplessly with the package's prefix; crossings still marked proposed keep the P prefix.
  4. Catchments and flow paths are recomputed, and renumber_log records every old → new ID with its status (unchanged, renumbered, new, deleted) and how far a moved point moved.

IDs change only when you run this tool. Run the report tool on the new package for an updated report.

33Known limitations

  • No tiling. The whole grid is processed in memory. Clip to the catchments plus a buffer, or use the auto-clip; results inside the clip are unchanged.
  • Flat terrain. On large flats the flat method decides the network and catchments switch between crossings under DEM error. QEHT flags this (flat check, uncertainty, divergence); it cannot resolve it. Survey or a better DEM can.
  • 30 m DEMs. Channels narrower than about three cells, road embankments and small culverts are not in the terrain. Channel sections often find the valley shoulder.
  • Proxies. The WorldCover-based curve numbers, C factors and Tc retardance values are matches, not measurements. Replace them with local lookups where they exist.
  • Single-threaded. The core is vectorised NumPy but uses one processor.
  • No hydraulics. QEHT stops at the catchment and its inputs: no design discharge, culvert sizing or flood levels.

34Quality-control checklist

  1. The DEM is one product, projected in metres, resampled bilinearly; the NoData and resampling audits are clean (or the NoData override is set).
  2. The stream threshold suits the smallest catchment you design for, and the snap threshold catches the tributaries the crossings sit on.
  3. Every crossing is reviewed against imagery or survey: accepted, rejected, moved or added; snap_dist_m is small.
  4. Flat-sensitive crossings (flat_sensitive = 1) and crossings that switch under DEM error are checked on a map or on site.
  5. Proposed crossings, flat stretches and mapped rivers without a crossing have each been decided.
  6. acc_at_outlet_km2 equals area_km2 for every full catchment.
  7. The design report states the slope used (and its field), the Tc method and its validity flag, the curve-number source and condition, and which values are proxies or estimates.
  8. The QEHT version, flat method and DEM SHA-256 from qeht_run_metadata are recorded with the design.
